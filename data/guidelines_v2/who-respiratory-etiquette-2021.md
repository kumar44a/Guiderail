## Scope
This guideline applies to patients, visitors and staff in any health facility, starting at the entrance.

## Recommendations
- Cover the mouth and nose with a tissue when coughing or sneezing. If no tissue is available, cough into the upper sleeve or elbow, not the hands.
- Dispose of used tissues in a waste bin immediately and clean hands afterwards.
- Offer a medical mask to patients with respiratory symptoms who are waiting in common areas.
- Seat patients with respiratory symptoms at least one metre away from others in waiting areas.

## Implementation
Post signs at entrances and waiting areas in the local languages. Provide tissues, no-touch waste bins and hand rub in every waiting area.

## Triage
Screen people for cough, fever and breathlessness at the entrance. Give a medical mask to anyone with symptoms and direct them to a separate waiting area. Staff at the entrance should wear a mask and keep one metre away where possible. Record the number screened each day.

## Masks
Show people how to wear a mask: cover the nose, mouth and chin and press the metal strip to the nose. Do not touch the front of the mask. Replace it when damp, and clean hands before putting on a fresh one. A worn mask is not hung under the chin.

## Ventilation
Open windows and doors in waiting areas where this is safe. Use fans to push air out, not across other people. Aim for at least 6 air changes per hour in waiting rooms. In rooms with mechanical ventilation, maintenance checks air flow every six months.

## Children and carers
Do not require young children under 2 years to wear a mask. A carer who accompanies a child with respiratory symptoms should wear a mask. Provide a separate play area for symptomatic children. Teach children to cough into their elbow using simple pictures.
