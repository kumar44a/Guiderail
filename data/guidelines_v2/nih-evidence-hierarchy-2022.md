## Scope
This document describes how study designs are ranked by their ability to limit bias when judging the effect of an intervention.

## Recommendations
- The hierarchy of clinical evidence, from strongest to weakest, is: systematic reviews of randomised trials, randomised controlled trials, cohort studies, case-control studies, case series and case reports, then expert opinion.
- Randomisation reduces selection bias and confounding, which is why randomised trials rank above observational studies.
- Use observational studies to assess harms that are rare or appear late, where trials are unlikely to be large or long enough.

## Notes
The hierarchy is a starting point and not a substitute for critical appraisal. A poorly conducted randomised trial can provide weaker evidence than a well-conducted cohort study.

## Systematic reviews
A systematic review uses explicit methods to find, select and appraise all relevant studies on a question. A meta-analysis is a statistical combination of results and may be part of a review. Reviews are only as strong as the studies in them and can mislead if they combine studies that differ greatly.

## Randomised controlled trials
In a randomised trial participants are allocated by chance to an intervention or a comparator. Concealing the allocation from those enrolling people prevents selection bias. Blinding reduces performance and detection bias. Trials that stop early for benefit may overestimate the effect.

## Observational designs
A cohort study follows groups defined by exposure over time. A case-control study compares people with a disease to people without it and looks back at exposures. Both are open to confounding, which is a mixing of the effects of different factors. Case series have no comparison group.

## Appraisal tools
Use a standard checklist for each design to judge the risk of bias. For trials, check randomisation, allocation concealment, blinding and loss to follow-up. Two reviewers should appraise each study independently and settle disagreements by discussion or by a third reviewer.
