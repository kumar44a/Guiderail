## Scope
This guidance covers storage, monitoring and transport of vaccines in clinics, pharmacies and hospitals.

## Recommendations
- Store refrigerated vaccines between 2 and 8 degrees Celsius. Store frozen vaccines between minus 50 and minus 15 degrees Celsius.
- Use a digital data logger with a buffered probe in every storage unit and read the temperature at the start and the end of each clinic day.
- Never store vaccines in the door of the unit or in produce drawers. Keep them in the centre, away from the walls and the cooling element.
- After a temperature excursion, move the affected vaccines to a working unit, label them as do not use, and contact the manufacturer for guidance before any are administered.
- Use qualified transport containers for off-site clinics and record the temperature during transport.

## Implementation
Name a primary and a backup vaccine coordinator. Connect storage units to backup power or have a written emergency plan. Keep temperature logs for at least three years.

## Storage unit set-up
Place the unit away from direct sunlight and heat sources, with at least 10 centimetres of clear space behind and beside it. Plug it into a dedicated outlet with a warning sign and a plug guard. Allow a newly installed unit to run for 2 to 3 days and record stable temperatures before stocking any vaccine.

## Daily monitoring
Record the current, minimum and maximum temperatures at the start of each clinic day and again before closing. Staff initial each entry. A reading outside the range is an excursion and must be acted on at once, not at the next scheduled check. Download the data logger report every week.

## Transport
For off-site clinics pack refrigerated vaccines in a qualified container with conditioned cold packs and a probe. Do not use dry ice for refrigerated vaccines. Keep the container out of direct sun and out of a vehicle boot. Record the temperature at departure, on arrival and every hour.

## Inventory
Place vaccines with the earliest expiry date at the front so they are used first. Do a stock count every month. Remove expired vaccine at once and keep it separate and labelled until it is disposed of. Keep diluents with their matching vaccine.
