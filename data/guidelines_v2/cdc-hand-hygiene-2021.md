## Scope
This guidance covers healthcare personnel in acute care, ambulatory and long-term care settings.

## Recommendations
- Alcohol-based hand rub is preferred in most clinical situations. Rub until hands are dry, which usually takes about 20 seconds.
- Use soap and water when hands are visibly dirty, and after caring for a patient with Clostridioides difficile or norovirus, because alcohol is less effective against spores and non-enveloped viruses.
- Clean hands before donning gloves and again after removing gloves.
- Do not wear artificial fingernails when in direct contact with patients at high risk, such as those in intensive care or the operating room.

## Implementation
Make hand rub available at every patient room entrance. Monitor adherence and give feedback to units monthly.

## Hand hygiene and gloves
Perform hand hygiene before putting on gloves for any invasive procedure and before handling a central line. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene because they can have small defects and hands can be contaminated during removal. Change gloves between tasks on the same patient when moving from a contaminated body site to a clean one.

## Skin care
Provide hospital-approved lotion to reduce dryness and dermatitis among staff. Do not use petroleum-based products that can weaken latex gloves. Staff with persistent skin damage should be referred to occupational health within 5 working days. Cuts and open wounds must be covered with a waterproof dressing before patient contact.

## Product selection
Choose hand rub containing 60 to 95 percent alcohol. Dispenser bottles must be single-use or cleaned and dried before refilling, and must never be topped up. Check expiry dates monthly. Discard any hand rub with no printed expiry date that is more than 12 months past its manufacture date.

## Monitoring and feedback
Use direct observation by trained auditors for at least 100 hand hygiene opportunities per unit each quarter. Share results with unit managers within 14 days. Units below 85 percent adherence should prepare an improvement plan and be audited again after 8 weeks.

## Outbreaks of Clostridioides difficile
During an outbreak of Clostridioides difficile, use soap and water for all care of affected patients and increase environmental cleaning. Display a sign at the door of each affected room. Continue the measure until 48 hours after diarrhoea has stopped.
