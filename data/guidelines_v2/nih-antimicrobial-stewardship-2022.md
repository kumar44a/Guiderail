## Scope
This document describes core elements of an antimicrobial stewardship programme in hospitals.

## Recommendations
- Document the indication, dose and planned duration in the record for every antibiotic prescription.
- Hold an antibiotic time-out at 48 to 72 hours to review the culture results and decide whether to continue, narrow or stop treatment.
- Choose the narrowest spectrum agent that is effective for the likely pathogen.
- Switch from intravenous to oral therapy as soon as the patient is clinically stable and able to absorb oral medication.
- Do not treat asymptomatic bacteriuria in patients who are not pregnant and are not about to have a urological procedure.

## Implementation
Provide prescribers with audit and feedback every quarter. Assign a physician and a pharmacist to lead the programme.

## Leadership and resources
The hospital executive must give protected time to the stewardship leads: at least 20 percent of a full-time pharmacist post for every 100 beds. Report outcomes to the hospital quality committee every quarter. Link prescribing data to the electronic health record so audit is not manual.

## Prospective audit and feedback
A pharmacist reviews all patients on restricted antibiotics every working day. When the review finds an inappropriate prescription, the pharmacist contacts the prescriber the same day and records the advice. Acceptance of the advice is tracked as a programme measure.

## Surgical prophylaxis
Give prophylactic antibiotic within 60 minutes before the incision. Stop it within 24 hours after surgery in most procedures. Do not continue prophylaxis only because a drain or catheter is still in place. Audit a sample of procedures each month.

## Education
Teach prescribers the local antibiogram every year. New doctors receive a stewardship briefing within their first two weeks. Provide pocket guidelines for common infections such as community-acquired pneumonia and urinary tract infection.
