## Scope
This document describes how clinics should detect, report and follow up adverse events after immunisation.

## Recommendations
- Report any serious adverse event, such as anaphylaxis, hospitalisation or death, within 24 hours of becoming aware of it.
- Report non-serious events within 7 days through the national adverse event reporting system.
- Record the vaccine product, lot number, date, site of administration and onset time for every report.
- Assess causality using a standard checklist and classify each event as consistent, indeterminate, inconsistent or unclassifiable.
- Follow up serious events for at least 30 days or until they resolve.

## Implementation
Name an adverse event focal point in each clinic. Review all reports at a monthly safety meeting and send summaries to the immunisation programme manager.

## What to report
Report any event that follows vaccination and that the patient, parent or clinician thinks may be related, even if the link is uncertain. Events that must always be reported include anaphylaxis, Guillain-Barre syndrome, seizures within 72 hours and any event that causes hospitalisation.

## The report form
Complete the form with the patient's age, sex, medical history, other medicines, the vaccine, dose number, the event description, onset date, treatment given and outcome. Do not leave fields blank: write unknown. Send a copy to the clinic's focal point.

## Causality assessment
Check whether the event follows vaccination at a plausible time, whether other causes exist, whether similar events are known for the vaccine and whether the event recurred on re-exposure. Record each answer. The assessment is repeated if new test results arrive.

## Communicating with families
Tell the family what is known about the event, what is being done and when they will hear again. Give a named contact and a phone number. Where an event is judged to be a true reaction, record it in the patient's record as an allergy or precaution.
