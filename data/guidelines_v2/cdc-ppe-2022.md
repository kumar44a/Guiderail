## Scope
This guidance applies to healthcare personnel who may be exposed to infectious agents while delivering care.

## Recommendations
- Choose PPE according to the anticipated exposure and the type of transmission precautions in place.
- Use an N95 or equivalent respirator for airborne precautions. Medical masks are sufficient for routine droplet precautions.
- Wear eye protection or a face shield when splashes or sprays are anticipated.
- Remove gloves first, then perform hand hygiene, then remove other PPE. The gown and respirator come off last.
- Do not wear the same gown between patients, even for extended use, unless the patients are cohorted for the same infection.

## Implementation
Annual respirator fit testing is required for staff who use tight-fitting respirators. Keep a log of fit-test results and retest after any facial change that could affect the seal.

## Respirator fit and seal checks
Perform a user seal check every time a tight-fitting respirator is put on. Facial hair that crosses the sealing surface prevents a good fit. Staff who cannot be fit tested should use a powered air-purifying respirator. Fit testing uses a qualitative or quantitative method and each worker is tested for the model and size they will use.

## Gowns
Use a fluid-resistant gown when splashes of blood or body fluids are likely. A disposable gown should be removed before leaving the patient room and thrown away in the room. Reusable cloth gowns must be laundered after each use. Do not use a gown that is torn or that does not cover the arms and torso.

## Extended use and shortages
During a shortage, extended use of the same respirator for repeated contacts with one cohort of patients may be allowed, up to 8 hours. Do not touch the front of the respirator. Discard it when it is damaged, soiled, wet or hard to breathe through.

## Training and observation
Train staff to put on and remove PPE before first use and every year. A trained observer should watch staff during donning and doffing in the first month after a new high-consequence pathogen is identified. Observers use a checklist and correct errors straight away.
