## Scope
This guideline applies to all health workers who provide direct patient care in hospitals, clinics and long-term care facilities.

## Recommendations
- Perform hand hygiene at five moments: before touching a patient, before a clean or aseptic procedure, after body fluid exposure risk, after touching a patient, and after touching patient surroundings.
- Use an alcohol-based hand rub for 15 to 20 seconds when hands are not visibly soiled.
- Wash with soap and water for 30 seconds when hands are visibly soiled or contaminated with blood or body fluids.
- Rings and artificial nails are discouraged in clinical areas.

## Implementation
Place hand rub dispensers at the point of care. Train all clinical staff at induction and annually. Audit compliance by direct observation twice a year.

## Technique
Apply a palmful of hand rub in a cupped hand. Rub the palms together, then the backs of the hands, between the fingers, the backs of the fingers, the thumbs and the fingertips. Continue until the hands are dry. Do not wipe or rinse after using hand rub.

## Jewellery and nails
Remove wrist watches and bracelets before patient care. A plain ring may be worn but should be moved to clean beneath it. Keep nails short, with tips less than 0.5 centimetres long. Artificial nails are discouraged because they harbour more organisms.

## Training and audit
Include hand hygiene in induction and repeat training every year. Audit compliance by direct observation twice a year in each ward. Give each ward its compliance rate and aim for a rise of 5 percentage points between audits.

## Supplies
Ensure each sink has liquid soap and single-use paper towels. Hand rub should be available at the point of care and in staff areas. Check dispensers weekly and record the check. Report any dispenser that is empty or broken within one working day.
