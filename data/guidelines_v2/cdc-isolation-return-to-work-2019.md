## Scope
This guidance applies to healthcare personnel who develop symptoms of an acute respiratory infection.

## Recommendations
- Staff with symptoms must stay away from work and must not provide patient care.
- Staff may return to work 10 days after symptom onset, provided they have been fever-free for at least 24 hours without fever-reducing medication and symptoms have improved.
- Staff who work with severely immunocompromised patients should be reassigned until they have completed the 10-day period.

## Implementation
Staff must report symptoms to occupational health before the start of their next shift. Occupational health keeps a record of absence dates and return dates.

## Reporting symptoms
Staff must tell their line manager and occupational health as soon as symptoms start, even if they are mild. A worker who becomes ill at work should leave patient areas at once, wear a medical mask while leaving, and go home. Do not use public transport if a private option exists.

## Occupational health assessment
Occupational health reviews every absence of more than 3 days before the worker returns. The review checks the date symptoms began, the date of the last fever, the present symptoms, and any contact with patients who are severely immunocompromised. The assessor signs a return-to-work form held in the employee file.

## Records
Keep a register of all respiratory illness absences by ward. Report the number of absent staff to the infection prevention team every Monday. A ward with 3 or more ill staff in one week should be reviewed for a possible cluster.

## Exceptions in staff shortage
When staffing is critically short, the director of nursing may ask occupational health to approve an earlier return for a worker who is fever-free and well. The worker must not care for severely immunocompromised patients and must wear a mask. Every exception is recorded with the reason.
