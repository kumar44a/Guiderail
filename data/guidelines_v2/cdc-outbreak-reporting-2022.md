## Scope
This guidance describes how facilities should recognise a possible outbreak and report it.

## Recommendations
- Treat two or more epidemiologically linked cases of the same infection within 14 days as a possible cluster and investigate.
- Notify the infection prevention team within 24 hours of identifying a possible cluster.
- Report confirmed outbreaks to the local health department within one working day.
- Build a line list of cases with onset date, location, exposures and outcome, and update it daily.
- Cohort affected patients and assign dedicated staff when possible.
- Declare the outbreak over after two maximum incubation periods have passed without a new case.

## Implementation
Convene an outbreak control team within 48 hours of confirming an outbreak and hold a review once it is over.

## Recognising a cluster
Look for clusters in the microbiology laboratory report, in ward handover notes and in staff illness records. Two cases of the same organism on one ward within 14 days should trigger a check of typing results. Compare resistance patterns, because an unusual pattern can reveal a cluster early.

## The line list
The line list should include patient identifier, age, ward, bed, onset date, specimen date, organism, symptoms, device use and outcome. Record the earliest onset date at the top. Review the list every morning and send an updated copy to the outbreak control team by 10:00.

## The outbreak control team
The team includes an infection prevention nurse, an infectious diseases physician, a microbiologist, a ward manager, an environmental services lead and a communications officer. The chair is the infection prevention physician. The team meets daily at first and twice a week once new cases slow.

## Communication
Tell patients and visitors on the affected ward what is happening, in plain language, within 24 hours of confirming an outbreak. Send a written update to all staff each Friday. Publish a short summary on the intranet once the outbreak is declared over.
