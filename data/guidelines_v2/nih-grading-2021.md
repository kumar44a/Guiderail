## Scope
This document describes how NIH-sponsored panels rate the body of evidence behind a clinical recommendation.

## Recommendations
- Rate the strength of evidence as high, moderate, low or insufficient.
- Use the rating insufficient when the evidence cannot be assessed because studies are missing, too small or contradictory.
- Assess each outcome separately and consider study design, directness, consistency and precision.
- Panels should record a recommendation as supported only when strength of evidence is at least moderate. Otherwise record it as a consensus statement.

## Notes
Consensus statements must be labelled as expert opinion and must not be presented as evidence-based recommendations.

## Domains for rating
Study design sets the starting level. Directness asks whether the studies match the population and outcome of interest. Consistency asks whether results agree across studies. Precision asks whether the confidence interval is narrow enough to support a decision. Each domain is rated and the panel records the reason.

## Strength of evidence categories
High means the panel is very confident that the true effect is close to the estimate. Moderate means further research may change the estimate. Low means further research is very likely to change it. Insufficient means no conclusion can be drawn.

## Panel process
A panel has at least seven members, including a methodologist and a patient representative. Members declare conflicts of interest before the first meeting. A recommendation is adopted when at least two thirds of members agree. Dissent is recorded in the minutes.

## Updating
Review the evidence for each recommendation every three years. Review sooner if a large trial or a safety signal is published. A recommendation that has not been reviewed within five years is marked as out of date on the website.
