## Scope
This guidance covers measures facilities should adopt between October and March when influenza and other respiratory viruses circulate widely.

## Recommendations
- Offer influenza vaccination to all staff by the end of October and track uptake by unit.
- Use universal masking in high-risk units, such as oncology and transplant, when community transmission is high.
- Screen visitors for respiratory symptoms at the entrance and restrict visitors who are symptomatic.
- Start antiviral treatment as early as possible, ideally within 48 hours of symptom onset, for patients at high risk of complications.
- Test patients with respiratory symptoms on admission and place them under transmission precautions while results are pending.

## Implementation
Review the facility surge plan before the season starts. Report weekly counts of respiratory admissions to the infection prevention committee.

## Staff vaccination
Offer influenza vaccine at work, at no cost, with extended clinic hours including night shifts. Track uptake by unit every two weeks from September. Staff who decline vaccination for non-medical reasons should wear a mask in patient areas during the influenza season and should sign a declination form.

## Visitor restrictions
When community transmission is high, limit each patient to two visitors at a time. Children under 12 should not visit units caring for infants, transplant patients or patients receiving chemotherapy. Visitors with symptoms are asked to stay home and may be offered a video call instead.

## Admission testing
Test every patient admitted with fever or respiratory symptoms for influenza, COVID-19 and respiratory syncytial virus using a multiplex test. Aim to report the result within 4 hours. Keep the patient in a single room until the result is available.

## Surge planning
Identify in advance which wards can be turned into respiratory cohort areas. Stock enough masks, gowns and test kits for 6 weeks of high demand. Hold a tabletop exercise in September with the emergency department, intensive care and the infection prevention team.
