## Scope
This guideline covers segregation, storage and transport of waste generated by health care activities.

## Recommendations
- Segregate waste at the point of generation into colour-coded containers: yellow for infectious waste, red for sharps in puncture-proof containers, and black for general waste.
- Do not mix infectious waste with general waste.
- Store waste in a secure, ventilated area for no longer than 48 hours in temperate climates and 24 hours in hot climates.
- Handlers must wear gloves, a gown or apron, boots and eye protection, and must be vaccinated against hepatitis B.

## Implementation
Use dedicated trolleys for waste transport and clean them daily. Keep a waste register and review volumes each month.

## Waste categories
Infectious waste includes materials contaminated with blood or body fluids. Sharps include needles, scalpels and broken glass. Pathological waste includes tissues and organs. Pharmaceutical waste includes expired or unused medicines. Chemical waste includes disinfectants and solvents. General waste is similar to household waste.

## Treatment and disposal
Autoclave or incinerate infectious waste at a licensed facility. Never burn waste in the open air. Sharps containers are sent for treatment when sealed. Bury pathological waste in a lined pit only where no other safe method exists. Record the quantity and method of treatment.

## Containers and labels
Use leak-proof containers with lids that close tightly. Label each container with the ward and the date. Change bags when they are two thirds full. Do not compress bags by hand or by foot, and never carry a bag against the body.

## Incidents
A spill of infectious waste is cleaned up immediately using gloves and 0.5 percent sodium hypochlorite. Report any injury to waste handlers on the incident form. Review incidents every month. Provide a hepatitis B vaccine booster as advised by occupational health.
