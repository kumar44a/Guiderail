## Scope
This guideline sets out how health workers should select, put on, take off and dispose of personal protective equipment (PPE).

## Recommendations
- Select PPE based on a risk assessment of the task, the patient and the likely exposure to blood, body fluids or respiratory droplets.
- Use gloves, a gown and eye protection when splashes or contact with body fluids are likely. Add a medical mask for droplet precautions and a particulate respirator for airborne precautions.
- Put on PPE in this order: gown, mask or respirator, eye protection, gloves.
- Take off PPE in this order: gloves, eye protection, gown, mask or respirator. Perform hand hygiene immediately after removal.
- Single-use gloves must never be reused or washed.

## Implementation
Fit-test every respirator user at first use and then once a year. Provide PPE at the point of use and display donning and doffing posters. Observe a sample of staff donning and doffing each quarter.

## Risk assessment
Before each task ask three questions: what is the task, what is the patient's infection status and what exposure is likely. Re-assess whenever the task changes. A risk assessment form should be on each ward and be reviewed every six months and after any incident.

## Masks and respirators
A medical mask protects against droplets and splashes. A respirator filters at least 94 percent of small airborne particles and is needed for tuberculosis, measles and aerosol-generating procedures. Cloth masks are not accepted as PPE in clinical care. Replace a mask when it becomes damp.

## Eye protection
Goggles must seal around the eyes. A face shield covers the whole face. Prescription glasses are not eye protection. Clean reusable eye protection with detergent and then disinfect it after each use, and store it dry.

## Supply and disposal
Keep a reserve of at least four weeks of PPE. Dispose of single-use PPE in a yellow infectious waste container. Place PPE stations outside isolation rooms and keep one doffing area with a hand hygiene point and a waste bin.
