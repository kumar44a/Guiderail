## Scope
This guidance covers prevention of needlestick and sharps injuries and the response after an exposure.

## Recommendations
- Use safety-engineered needles and devices whenever they are available.
- Do not recap used needles. If recapping is unavoidable, use a one-handed scoop technique.
- Place sharps in a puncture-resistant container at the point of use and replace the container when it is three quarters full.
- After an injury, wash the wound with soap and water, report the incident immediately to your supervisor and occupational health.
- Begin post-exposure assessment as soon as possible, ideally within 2 hours, and arrange baseline blood testing for the exposed worker and, with consent, the source patient.

## Implementation
Review all sharps injuries quarterly to find trends. Provide training on safe use of devices to every new employee and when a new device is introduced.

## Safe handling during procedures
Pass sharps with a tray or neutral zone, not from hand to hand. Announce when a sharp is being passed. Keep the sharp pointing away from yourself and others. Never leave a needle on a bed or in a pocket, and never bend or break a needle by hand.

## Sharps containers
Mount containers at standing eye level where staff can see the opening. Never push sharps down with your hand, and never reach into a container. Seal the container when it is three quarters full. Containers are collected for treatment and are not opened or emptied.

## After an exposure
Let the wound bleed freely and wash with soap and water for several minutes. Flush a splash to the eye with clean water or saline for at least 15 minutes. Do not squeeze the wound or apply bleach. Report the injury, the device and how it happened on the incident form.

## Prophylaxis and follow-up
If the source patient is known to have HIV, begin antiretroviral prophylaxis within 2 hours if possible and no later than 72 hours. Follow-up testing is done at 6 weeks, 3 months and 6 months. Offer counselling to the exposed worker at each visit.
