## Scope
This guidance sets out vaccines recommended for healthcare personnel and the practice steps for giving them.

## Recommendations
- Give influenza vaccine every year to all healthcare personnel.
- Give one dose of Tdap, then a Td or Tdap booster every 10 years.
- Vaccinate healthcare personnel against hepatitis B and check anti-HBs levels 1 to 2 months after the final dose of the series.
- Confirm documented immunity to measles, mumps and rubella and to varicella, and give vaccine to staff without evidence of immunity.
- Screen every recipient for contraindications and precautions before vaccination.
- Observe recipients for 15 minutes after vaccination, and for 30 minutes if they have a history of severe allergic reaction.

## Implementation
Record each dose in the immunisation registry and in the employee health record. Keep epinephrine and trained staff available wherever vaccines are given.

## Hepatitis B
A hepatitis B series has three doses, given at 0, 1 and 6 months. Test anti-HBs 1 to 2 months after the last dose. A worker whose level is below 10 mIU/mL should repeat the series once and be tested again. Staff who still do not respond are considered non-responders and need counselling about exposures.

## Measles, mumps and rubella
Acceptable evidence of immunity is written documentation of two doses of vaccine, laboratory evidence of immunity, or laboratory confirmation of the disease. Birth before 1957 is not by itself evidence of immunity for staff in healthcare. Give two doses at least 28 days apart to those without evidence.

## Varicella
Staff are immune if they have two documented doses of vaccine, a laboratory result showing immunity or a diagnosis of varicella made by a healthcare provider. Staff without evidence receive two doses 4 to 8 weeks apart. Non-immune staff are restricted from duty from day 8 to day 21 after an exposure.

## Contraindications and precautions
Severe allergy to a vaccine component is a contraindication to that vaccine. Moderate or severe acute illness is a precaution and vaccination is usually delayed. Live vaccines are not given in pregnancy or to people who are severely immunocompromised. Document the reason whenever a vaccine is deferred.
