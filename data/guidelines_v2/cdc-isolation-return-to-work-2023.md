## Scope
This guidance applies to healthcare personnel who develop symptoms of an acute respiratory infection. It replaces the 2019 edition.

## Recommendations
- Staff with symptoms must stay away from work and must not provide patient care.
- Staff may return to work 5 days after symptom onset if they have been fever-free for at least 24 hours without fever-reducing medication and symptoms are improving.
- Staff must wear a well-fitting mask in all patient care areas through day 10 after symptom onset.
- Staff who work with severely immunocompromised patients should have a negative test result on day 5 or later before returning, or remain off work until day 10.

## Changes from the 2019 edition
The minimum period before return fell from 10 days to 5 days. Masking through day 10 and a test requirement for staff who work with immunocompromised patients were added.

## Reporting symptoms
Staff must tell their line manager and occupational health as soon as symptoms start, even if they are mild. A worker who becomes ill at work should leave patient areas at once, wear a medical mask while leaving, and go home. Counting of days begins on the day symptoms start, which is day 0.

## Testing
A rapid antigen test is acceptable for the day 5 test. If the first test on day 5 is positive, repeat it every 48 hours until negative or until day 10. A worker with a positive test on day 10 must stay off work and ask occupational health to review.

## Masking through day 10
The mask must be a medical mask or a respirator that fits well with no gaps. Staff may remove it only when eating or drinking alone, away from patients and colleagues. Masking stops after day 10 even if mild symptoms such as a cough continue.

## Records and exceptions
Occupational health records the onset date, return date, test result and masking plan for every absence. A ward manager may ask for an earlier return only during a declared staffing emergency, and the decision is signed by the director of nursing and logged.
