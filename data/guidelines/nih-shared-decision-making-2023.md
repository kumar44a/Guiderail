## Scope
This document defines shared decision making and describes how clinicians should apply it when more than one reasonable option exists.

## Recommendations
- Shared decision making is a process in which clinicians and patients work together to choose tests, treatments or care plans, based on clinical evidence and on the patient's values and preferences.
- Follow six steps: invite the patient to participate, present the options, discuss benefits and harms, elicit the patient's preferences, reach a decision together, and revisit the decision when circumstances change.
- Use decision aids written in plain language when they are available for the condition.
- Document the options discussed, the patient's stated preferences and the final decision in the record.

## Notes
Shared decision making is most useful when evidence is uncertain or when options carry different trade-offs. It is not required for emergency care where the patient cannot take part.
