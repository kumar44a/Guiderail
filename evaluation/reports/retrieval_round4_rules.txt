Retrieval round 4. Written and committed BEFORE any round-4 change or measurement. It continues round 3 (retrieval_round3_rules.txt).

Round 3 outcome (see retrieval_round3_*.json): only B (hybrid BM25 + FAISS fusion) was kept. A (compound replacement) lowered synthetic MRR
and did nothing for real dev; C (query expansion) and D (per-section reference removal) produced no measurable gain alone.
The owner's four tried questions (hand rub duration, benefits of hand rub, where to find hand rub) still fail under B.

Why these are retried: round 3 judged each change alone against a baseline that had none of the others. The failing questions need
several things at once. Example: "how long should i rub my hands with sanitiser" must reach a chunk that says "How to handrub ...
Duration of the entire procedure: 20-30 seconds". That needs handrub ~ hand rub (A), long ~ duration (C) and sanitiser ~ handrub (C).
So the changes are tried again in a different form, on top of B, in this order, each against the previous kept state.
This is a second look at the same dev set, which raises the risk of fitting it. The sealed set remains the arbiter.

Changes:
  A2  Compound handling that KEEPS both spellings instead of replacing one. Applied to documents and questions before tokenising.
      The pattern  hand[ -]*(rub|wash)(suffix)  is rewritten to emit both forms side by side, for example
        "hand rub"  -> "handrub hand rub"       "handrubbing" -> "handrubbing hand rubbing"       "hand-washing" -> "handwashing hand washing"
      and  health[ -]*care -> "healthcare health care".  A document that says "handrub" thus also contains hand and rub, and a
      question that says "hand rub" also contains handrub; the two-word pair (bigram) survives for texts that use it.
  C   The same query-side expansion table as in round 3, unchanged (see retrieval_round3_rules.txt), with the same weights.
  D2  Reference-list removal by RUNS. A section is citation-like when it has at least 2 non-empty rows (its title counts) and at least half
      of them are citation lines (same citation-line definition as round 3). Maximal runs of 3 or more consecutive citation-like sections
      are dropped. Isolated citation-like sections stay.

Data, metrics, guards G1-G5 and the keep rule are exactly those of round 3, evaluated on the same real-dev and synthetic-dev material,
using the extractive generator, against the previous kept state (round 3's B for A2).
After the last kept change: freeze, run dev end to end for both generators, then the sealed set once per generator, together with the
questions the owner writes in evaluation/user_questions.txt. Everything is reported as found. Nothing is tuned on the sealed set.

ADDENDUM 1 (written after A2 was measured and rejected, and after D2 was implemented but BEFORE any retrieval measurement of D)
A2 failed every guard and is reverted. C is unchanged from round 3 (A2 was its only new context), so its round-3 verdict stands: revert.
D2 as written cannot work: it drops only runs of 3+ fragmented sections, but the WHO reference list is mostly a few very long
sections (one heading, many entries), and each entry spans several lines of which only about a third match a citation pattern.
This was found by reading the documents and my own test fixture, not from any retrieval number. D2 is replaced by D3 before measuring:
  D3  A section is dropped when either
        (a) it has at least 6 non-empty rows (title counts) and at least 30% of them are citation lines, or
        (b) it belongs to a maximal run of 3 or more consecutive sections that each have at least 2 rows with at least 30% citation lines.
      The citation-line definition is unchanged.
Calibration, on document content only (no retrieval result used): list every dropped section's title and page for all five real PDFs and
check by eye that each is reference or author-list material and that nothing from the body of a guideline is dropped.
The retrieval measurement of D3 then follows the usual keep rule against the round-3 kept state.
